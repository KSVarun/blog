## Why does the bar get so thin?

A washing bar doesn't wear evenly. When you rub it on wet fabric, the surface softens into a slimy gel and gets scraped away. The center gets the most contact, so it wears fastest. Sitting in a puddle of water makes it worse, because the bar keeps absorbing water and turning to mush. Once the bar is thin, it can't be gripped and it snaps under pressure. Most people give up on it at that point, even though 10 to 15% of the soap may be left.

## Method 1: Crushing it into powder (and why it often fails)

Crushing seems logical, but coarse bits barely dissolve. A laundry bar is pressed dense and packed with hardening agents and fillers like sodium carbonate and silicates. Rough chunks have very little surface area touching the water, and cold water makes it worse, since soap dissolves slowly when cold.

The fix is to **grate it fine and dissolve it in hot water** instead of adding dry bits to the wash. Grating gives you thin shavings with lots of surface area. Hot water breaks down the compact structure quickly. Stir the shavings into hot water and let them sit, and you get a soap gel or liquid that mixes into the wash instantly.

## Method 2: Sticking the sliver onto a new wet bar (why it works so well)

This is the neatest trick, and the reason it works is basic soap chemistry.

Soap molecules have a water-loving head and a fat-loving tail. When a bar is wet, its surface absorbs water and turns into a soft, sticky layer of partially dissolved soap. Press a thin piece onto a wet new bar and the two soft layers mingle and interlock at the molecular level. As the bar dries, water evaporates and the dissolved soap recrystallizes, **fusing the two pieces into one solid bar**, much like a weld.

**For the best bond:**

- Wet both surfaces, but don't soak them.
- Press firmly for 10 to 15 seconds.
- Let it dry for 12 to 24 hours before use, so the joint fully hardens.

## More ways to save every bit

- **Keep the bar dry between uses.** Rest it on a draining rack or a slanted dish so it never sits in water. This alone can extend its life by a lot.
- **Cut a new bar in half.** A smaller bar is easier to hold, and you'll waste less as it thins.
- **Use a brush.** Rub the brush on the bar and scrub the fabric. This wears the bar more evenly and avoids the thin-middle problem.
- **Use a mesh bag.** Put slivers in a small mesh pouch or an old sock and toss it into the wash

## The takeaway

A soap sliver isn't waste, it's just soap in an awkward shape. Grate and dissolve it, fuse it onto a fresh bar, or bag it, and you'll get noticeably more washes out of every bar.
